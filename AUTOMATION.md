# Job Radar Update Contract

## Sources and scope

- Scan these sources every day, in this order:
  1. **Email lead source:** Before the repository scan, use the connected Gmail account to search the previous calendar day's SWELIST messages. Run separate sender queries for `noreply@swelist.com` and `noreply=swelist.com@mg.swelist.com`, bounded to the previous calendar day in `America/Los_Angeles`; deduplicate matching messages before reading them. Read the complete body of every matching message and extract job links, but do not mark messages read, archive, label, delete, reply, or otherwise mutate the mailbox.
  2. **Primary:** `NEW_GRAD_USA.md` from `speedyapply/2027-SWE-College-Jobs`.
  3. **Supplemental:** `vanshb03/New-Grad-2027` on its current default branch. It contains substantial 2025/2026 history; consider only a new or changed role with explicit 2027 graduation or start-cycle evidence.
  4. **Monitor:** `SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions` and the `SimplifyJobs` organization. Check both whether the existing repository has explicitly switched to 2027 and whether a new repository explicitly targeting 2027, New Grad, or College Jobs exists. Do not add 2026 positions merely because Simplify has updated.
- Treat SWELIST as a discovery lead, not as official eligibility evidence. Ignore newsletter navigation, tracking, unsubscribe, internship, and non-job links; resolve each candidate to its normalized official application URL and then apply the same official-page verification, scope, and dedupe rules used for repository candidates. A suitable open new-grad or early-career role without official 2027 timing may enter as `timing-check`; silence never confirms timing or sponsorship.
- Never persist the Gmail account, recipient address, sender metadata, subject, message/thread IDs, email body, or mailbox labels in this repository, the site, source state, logs, or daily report. Only public job URLs and public job facts may flow into the candidate dataset. `source-state.json` may retain only a generic SWELIST source status, check time, and normalized public candidate URLs.
- If no matching SWELIST email exists, record zero email leads and continue. If Gmail access or authentication fails, mark only the email source `needs-review`, report it, and continue the repository scan without guessing or treating the mailbox failure as an empty result.
- Scope: Discover United States, full-time SWE, AI/ML, ML Systems, Data, Backend, Infra, Quant Developer, or Quant Research roles with source age `<= 60d`. Include roles with explicit 2027 timing as eligible; an otherwise open and directionally suitable early-career or new-grad role without official 2027 timing may be shown as `timing-check` / pending. Never infer its start date or treat it as confirmed. Retention cleanup is temporarily paused by user instruction, so existing cards older than 60 days remain visible until the user re-enables cleanup.
- Exclude roles whose core work is low-level Linux or operating-systems engineering, kernel or OS internals, Linux fleet administration, networking or storage systems, package management such as Nix/RPM, or production infrastructure operations that depend primarily on those skills. Do not exclude ML Systems, CUDA/GPU performance, distributed training, Backend, or Data Infra merely because Linux appears as a normal development environment; apply this exclusion only when low-level Linux/OS work is central to the role.
- When the site already retains an Engineering, Quant Developer, or Quant Research role from the same company, exclude that company's pure Trader/Trading role. Keep hybrid Algorithm Developer or Quant Research & Trading roles, and keep a pure trading role when no retained same-company alternative exists.
- Treat `sourceMonitoring.preferenceExclusions` as the durable public-URL denylist for user preference exclusions. Do not re-add a listed role merely because another source mentions it or its source age changes. Reconsider a low-level systems exclusion only when the official responsibilities materially change; reconsider a pure-trading exclusion when the retained same-company Engineering, Quant Developer, or Quant Research alternative is no longer active; also reconsider any entry when the user changes the relevant preference.
- Treat `sourceMonitoring.hardEligibilityExclusions` as the durable public-URL denylist for roles whose official page confirms a hard exclusion such as no employer sponsorship, citizenship/clearance restrictions, or an incompatible cohort. Keep only the public URL and public exclusion reason; never place user work-authorization or identity data in this list. Re-evaluate an entry only after the official requisition or its relevant eligibility language materially changes.
- `data/source-state.json` is the shared scan baseline. Preserve the legacy SpeedyApply fields used by the site and update `sourceMonitoring` for all four sources on every run: each source's public configuration, cycle/monitoring state, last-check time, candidate-only baseline, and source mentions. Keep uncertain cross-source matches in `suspectedDuplicates`; do not create a duplicate job card.
- Use an incremental daily scan. For SpeedyApply, process only roles added or materially changed since the last successful baseline: a normalized official URL not yet in `seenCanonicalUrls`, a known official job ID whose link/title/location changed, or a row identified as changed by the source diff. In normal daily runs, do not reopen or re-screen unchanged baseline rows from the previous 60 days.
- The `age <= 60d` rule remains the discovery boundary, not the daily discovery window. Advance the displayed source age for retained cards, but do not remove cards solely for exceeding 60 days while the temporary retention pause is active. Re-open an existing official page only when the source reports a material change, closure/reopening, the saved link fails, or the user requests a review.
- A full 60-day backfill/audit is exceptional and must run only when the user explicitly asks for a rescan, omission audit, or baseline rebuild. If the baseline is missing or corrupt during an ordinary daily run, stop and report `needs-review`; do not silently substitute a full-window scan.
- Candidate discovery for the incremental rows must inspect general titles and official degree/experience language, not rely only on `New Grad`, `Associate`, `Junior`, or `Engineer I` keywords.
- Store `directions` using exactly these canonical public tags: `Quant`, `AI/ML`, `ML Systems`, and `SWE/Data Infra`. Classify from the official role's core responsibilities. Never add `Quant` merely because a Quant resume could be used, the employer operates in finance, or the role uses statistical or machine-learning methods; `Quant` requires trading, market, pricing, portfolio/risk, quantitative finance/economics, or quant-firm engineering responsibilities. Resume-track selection is downstream metadata and must not broaden the direction filter.
- Give every discovered public candidate a terminal or retryable disposition before its source URL becomes suppressible by the daily baseline: `admitted`, `hard-excluded`, `preference-excluded`, `out-of-scope`, `duplicate`, or `needs-review`. A URL appearing in a source `seen` set is discovery history, not proof that it was screened. Unresolved `needs-review` and `duplicate` candidates remain retryable and must carry public `firstSeenAt`, `lastAttemptAt`, and `retryAfter` timestamps; do not permanently suppress them merely because the source row is unchanged.
- Preserve baseline continuity. A successful scan may union newly observed public URLs into source history, but must not silently replace that history with only the newest newsletter or repository snapshot. If a baseline count shrinks without an explicit source-removal ledger, mark the source `needs-review` and do not publish the rewritten baseline.
- Update `data/jobs.json` for recommended or technically strong roles. Required fields: `id`, `canonicalUrl`, `company`, `role`, `location`, `ageDays`, `source`, `firstSeenAt`, `lastCheckedAt`, `eligibility`, `startTiming`, `sponsorship`, `fitTier`, `fitScore`, `directions`, `resumeTrack`, `reasons`, `caveats`, `applyUrl`, `status`.
- Update `data/source-state.json` on every scan. Preserve `firstSeenAt`; set scan timestamps and append every scanned normalized URL to the appropriate source baseline. During the temporary retention pause, remove a listed job only after it is confirmed closed or fails a hard eligibility rule, not merely because its source age exceeds 60 days.
- Dedupe across all sources and the unified application record in this order: official job ID; normalized official application URL (remove tracking parameters, fragments, and trailing locale variants); company + role + location; then company + similar title + highly similar responsibilities. Update a matching existing record when a link changes or reopens; only unresolved matches go to `suspectedDuplicates` for main-thread review.
- Open every candidate's official job page to verify responsibilities, cohort/start timing, location, open status, and sponsorship language. Never infer 2027 timing or sponsorship from silence: use `timing-check` and `unknown` until official evidence exists. Do not include a closed role, a role limited to U.S. citizens, a role that declines future sponsorship, or a role with stated experience or degree timing incompatible with the user's graduation plan.
- Treat an isolated official-page access failure as a candidate-level verification failure, not a batch-wide publication failure. Hold that candidate out of `jobs.json`, retain only its normalized public URL and a public `needs-review` reason in source state, and continue processing independently verified candidates. Fail the whole batch only when the official-page or network failure is systemic enough that the bounded candidate set cannot be verified reliably; never use a mirror or source lead to guess missing official facts.
- Keep private profile data out of both JSON files.
- Derive `data/applications.json` from the unified application record. Keep only `id`, `company`, optional `role`, and one simple status: `applying`, `needs-review`, `submitted`, or `paused`. The site's private D1 store may additionally use `skipped` for user-selected “不投递” jobs. Do not copy answers, dates, resume versions, notes, or sensitive fields into the site.

Map `started`, `analyzing`, and `filling` to `applying`; map `needs-review` and `ready-to-submit` to `needs-review`; preserve `submitted`; map `blocked` to `paused`. Never overwrite a D1 `skipped` choice during a daily data refresh; only the user's restore action removes it.
